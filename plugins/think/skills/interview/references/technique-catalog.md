# Interview — technique catalog + phase detail + sufficiency

Read when drilling (Phase 2) or deciding whether to stop. The four techniques are the interviewer's own —
compose no other skill to run them.

## Drilling techniques

### 5-Whys
- **When:** an answer asserts something without a reason ("we switched because it was better").
- **How:** "Why do you hold that?" → answer → "and why is *that* true?" — down to a concrete event or decision, max ~5 levels.
- **Stop:** you reach a specific moment or root cause, not another abstraction.

### Scene
- **When:** an answer is abstract, with no sensory or temporal detail.
- **How:** "Walk me through one concrete moment. Where were you? What was on the screen? What happened next?"
- **Stop:** a reader could *picture* the moment.

### Devil's advocate
- **When:** the expert is confident but has not articulated why, or a claim stands unchallenged.
- **How:** "A sharp critic would counter that <counter drawn from the grounding map>. What do you tell them?"
- **Stop:** the expert gives a reasoned rebuttal, not a dismissal.

### What-if-not
- **When:** a story or decision with no stakes.
- **How:** "What would have happened if you hadn't? Or had done the opposite?"
- **Stop:** the stakes are concrete.

## Phase detail

- **Phase 0 — Ground & map.** Ingest reachable evidence + handed-in precedent → grounding map (consensus,
  camps, open questions, key data, where the expert may diverge). None reachable → say so; run ungrounded.
- **Phase 1 — Recon.** 3–4 questions to locate the expert vs the map; find the 1–2 thinnest / most divergent points.
- **Phase 2 — Drill.** 5–8 adaptive questions; pick the technique per the weakness; never accept the first answer; switch techniques.
- **Phase 3 — Crystallize.** Play back summary + the core sentence; confirm/correct; unfilled slots → back to Phase 2 (bounded by L1).

## The seven slots

`core` (the one sentence) · `claim` (the thesis/position) · `counter` (the counter-position + the expert's
rebuttal) · `evidence` (a concrete case/scene with specifics) · `data` (numbers/sources) · `insight` (the
non-obvious "thought X, found Y") · `so-what` (the actionable takeaway). **L2-required subset:** core + ≥1
claim + ≥1 evidence + so-what. Everything else may stay unfilled — but only with an explicit flag in the output.

## Sufficiency — three levels, priority L3 > L1 > L2

- **L3 — expert signals (highest).** Answers shortening across 3+ turns, repetition, "enough"/"that's all",
  repeated "don't know" → wind down honestly to Phase 3. The expert's signal outranks any counter.
- **L1 — hard limits (mechanical).** Max ~15 questions total · max 3 clarifiers on one topic · max 2
  Phase-3→2 returns. Reaching a limit forces the move to Phase 3.
- **L2 — minimum slots (lowest).** The L2-required subset filled + a core sentence the expert confirms.
  Sufficient to emit; unfilled optional slots are flagged, not chased.

Priority means: an L3 signal ends the interview even if L1 limits are not reached and L2 slots are not full —
better to under-collect than to grind the expert into disengagement.
